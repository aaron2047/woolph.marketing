01

Executive summary

Dr. Jones has two strong assets: a credible, high-level story (U.S. Army veteran, former Department of Defense professional, founder of Interactive Advocacy, President of NCVETBIZ, creator of the GLOW Framework) and a working booking pipeline on the Interactive Advocacy site. The current web presence does not yet turn that story into inbound engagements, especially for the Policy & Leadership Forum work the personal brand is built to promote.

Three findings matter most:

  1. The Policy & Leadership Forum offer is under-built. It appears as a single sentence on the Speaking Engagements page. There is no dedicated page, topic list, past-convening proof, or forum-specific inquiry path.
  2. The two sites send mixed signals. The personal site presents a governance and humanity thought leader. The Interactive Advocacy site presents a violence-prevention training company that currently advertises pro-bono engagements. Visitors pass between them with no clear logic.
  3. Trust details are leaking. Past events shown as upcoming, placeholder social links, an email on a different domain spelling, inconsistent impact numbers, and varying title wording all weaken confidence with buyers who verify before they book.

Recommended direction: make the personal site the front door for keynotes and forums, give the Forum offer its own page and inquiry path, and keep Interactive Advocacy as the training-and-performance arm with a clear, labeled link between the two.

02

Scope and method

Reviewed: both homepages in full. On drtanyaojones.com, the About, Work & Impact, Speaking Engagements, Insights, and Contact pages. On interactiveadvocacy.com, the Speaker Request, Book Online, Contact, and founder bio pages. Not reviewed: Shop, Store, Videos, Sponsorship, FAQ, and individual blog posts.

Technical checks: a full Lighthouse run could not be completed because Google's public PageSpeed service was over its usage quota. The figures in Section 06 come from in-browser measurements of page structure and loading and are indicative only. A formal mobile Lighthouse report is the first recommended follow-up.

Not verified: the live status of any Google Business Profile, review counts, and search rankings. Both sites run on Wix.

07

Consistency and credibility

Buyers in government and international settings verify claims. Each item below should be reconciled and linked to its source before any relaunch:

  • Impact numbers differ. The personal site says programs reached more than 80,000 individuals; the Interactive Advocacy bio says over 50 thousand trained.
  • Title wording differs. The personal site says "United Nations Peacekeeping Ambassador." The Interactive Advocacy bio says "Ambassador for Peace," conferred February 4, 2025, and separately cites ECOSOC special consultative status. Use one verified phrasing, name the conferring body, and make the hero title match.
  • Awards need sources. The Presidential Lifetime Achievement Award, Global Icon recognitions, and leadership awards should each link to a program page, photo, or certificate.
  • Contact details are inconsistent. One phone number serves both brands. A third-party business directory lists a different Interactive Advocacy address (Concord, NC) than the website (Charlotte, NC) and shows no reviews.
  • A 2024 press release lists a third website, leadershipexperiencetour.com. Please confirm whether it belongs to Dr. Jones.

08

Google Business Profile and reputation

Where it helps most

For Interactive Advocacy, a Charlotte-based company with a street address and weekday hours, a verified Google Business Profile is a worthwhile source of regional and procurement leads. It needs a consistent name, address, and phone, service areas, services, photos, and reviews.

Where it helps less

Event planners looking for a keynote or forum speaker mostly search by topic and check speaker bureaus, LinkedIn, and video. For the personal brand, a profile is a supporting asset. The primary engine is a Forum-focused site with a reel, proof, structured data, and a fast inquiry path. Eligibility for a speaker profile depends on Google's rules and needs checking first.

Before any profile work

  • Confirm that the Suite 400 location is a staffed location. Google can suspend profiles that appear to use virtual offices.
  • Confirm which address is current and align every listing.
  • Check whether a profile already exists or has been claimed.

Reputation tools worth considering

  • A post-event message that asks every host and attendee for a review, without filtering who is asked (Google prohibits review gating).
  • A testimonial intake form with permission capture, publishing approved quotes on the site.
  • A review dashboard and monthly summary.

09

Recommended direction

The strongest response is a purpose-built personal site designed around the Forum offer, built on a modern stack (Next.js, React, Tailwind CSS) so it is fast, searchable, and easy to extend with booking and lead tools.

Page plan

  • Home: positioning, reel, proof strip, one primary action
  • Policy & Leadership Forums: formats, topics, outcomes, past convenings, host FAQ, inquiry form
  • Keynotes and Panels, with one-sheet downloads
  • Work & Impact: verified credentials and media, with source links
  • Events: archives past events automatically, with Event schema
  • Insights: dated articles
  • Book: qualifying inquiry form and discovery-call scheduling
  • Press kit: bio, headshots, introduction, one-sheet
  • Capability statement page for procurement buyers

Built-in tools

  • Inquiry form with routing and instant confirmation
  • Lead capture and follow-up
  • Review requests and testimonial intake
  • Analytics showing which pages produce inquiries
